The problem

Drowning in paper

Letters, filings, exhibits, emails. A live dispute produces hundreds of documents, and the one you need is always the one you can't find. Without a system, every search starts from zero — the night before it's due.

Missed dates

Orders set dates. Rules set dates. Registries set dates. Miss one and you may be explaining yourself to a judge, or asking for indulgences you didn't need to ask for. A date you didn't write down is a date you're trusting to memory.

The registry expects order

Courts run on structure: numbered paragraphs, indexed bundles, precise references. A self-represented party who files organized, findable material is easier to deal with — and easier to take seriously.

What's in the pack

Six tools. Each one exists to stop a specific, common way cases go wrong.

01Chronology template

One dated line per event, with its source document. Prevents the scramble to reconstruct what happened, in what order, when a deadline is already running.

02Evidence index

Every document numbered, described and located the day it arrives. Prevents the exhibit you know exists but cannot produce when it matters.

03Correspondence log

Who said what to whom, on what date, by what channel. Prevents the dispute about what was sent and when — you'll have the row, they'll have their recollection.

04Deadline & orders tracker

Every date from every order and rule in one list, with what it requires and its status. Prevents the quiet deadline that passes while you're busy with a loud one.

05Hearing-prep checklist

What to bring, what to file beforehand, what to have marked up and to hand. Prevents standing at the lectern realising the page you need is at home.

06Filing-system guide

A plain naming and folder scheme that scales from ten documents to a thousand. Prevents the folder called "Court stuff" that nobody — including you — can navigate.
